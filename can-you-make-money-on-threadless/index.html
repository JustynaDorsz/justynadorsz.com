How to make money on Threadless

What I’ve learned from selling my designs on Threadless for years.

  1. Make a lot of designs. You never know which one is going to be popular.
  2. Don’t get discouraged when no one buys your designs at first. Just keep designing and publishing.
  3. Try to find your niche. I make a lot of designs with cats and space because that’s what I like to draw. Maybe you like dogs or hiking or pizza? Draw that.
  4. Don’t judge your own work too harshly. Some of my best-selling designs are ones I thought no one would like. Publish them and see what happens.
  5. Funny designs sell better. Whenever someone says something funny, write it down. It might become your next design.
  6. Check if your design also works with the colors reversed. Some people only buy light t-shirts, some only dark ones, so offer both: black on white and white on black.
  7. Make sure most of the pixels in your design are fully opaque. Printers used for apparel don’t print semi-transparent areas well, and they may disappear on the shirt.
  8. Tags! Add tags to your designs so people can find them. I try to add at least 8 tags to each design. It’s a good idea to also write a short description.
  9. When you add a design to your store make sure you enable it on other products, not only t-shirts. Customers often buy hoodies, masks, tote bags, and pillows. In my shop people also buy leggings, stickers, magnets, and even beach towels.
  10. Submit designs both to your Artist Shop and to Design Challenges. Even if you don’t win a challenge, more people will see your designs and you will gain new followers.
  11. In your Artist Shop settings, allow your shop’s products to be considered in the Threadless Marketplace. Your shop is separate from the main Threadless store, and without this your designs won’t show up there.
  12. Post about your new designs on Instagram, Twitter, Tumblr, Facebook. The more people see your designs the better. But don’t worry if you are not good at marketing: almost all of my sales come from people who find my designs on Threadless.
  13. Look after your store. Every now and then go through your designs: update tags, enable new products, improve descriptions, add color variants, and put new designs into collections.
  14. Expect the seasons. November and December are the best months, January and February the slowest. Sales always pick up again in spring.
  15. Take part in site-wide sales. You earn less per item during a sale, but many more items are sold, so you earn more overall. Why a 50% off sale doesn’t punish an artist.
  16. If you haven’t yet opened a store, you should open one now! I have no affiliation with Threadless, I just like them a lot and earn a lot of money there. And don’t spend too long on the name — the work matters more.
